UE5M3 FP4 block scaling for stable pretraining

Hu, Luschi, and Balanca pair E2M1 FP4 payloads with unsigned E5M3 block scales so periodic tensor scaling can replace NVIDIA Transformer Engine NVFP4's current-tensor scaling, randomized Hadamard transform, and BF16 final layers. They pretrain Nemotron-H 8B for nearly 190 billion tokens and report lower final-window training loss and lower held-out NLL than the NV recipe under each side's quantized-inference policy. A native NV ablation that drops RHT and the BF16 exemption raises measured model-body token throughput 21.2%. The paper is a software-emulated recipe that argues for native UE5M3 block-scale support.

Ax Shi, Sun, Dong, Wan, Lo, Gu arXiv › Recommended

EarlyEval: cheaper agent evaluation via early outcome prediction

Shi and colleagues train LightGBM success and failure classifiers on behavioral, textual, and reference-solution features and halt an agent run once either crosses a calibrated threshold. Across SWE-bench Verified, TerminalBench, and Toolathlon they cut 13–26% of agent steps and up to 44.1% input tokens and 29.4% output tokens at 89–97% prediction accuracy. Per-agent resolve rates move only one to two points on average. The point is to cheapen each retained task rather than distill the benchmark down to fewer tasks.

Gh 2akouwu / GitHub GitHub › Recommended

reverify: AI reverse engineering judged by the binary

reverify is an MIT CLI and MCP server that lets a model propose claims about a PE, ELF, or Mach-O binary and has a deterministic toolkit return VERIFIED, REFUTED, or INCONCLUSIVE with the bytes it actually read. v0.5.0 adds behavior_equiv: run the original function and a reconstruction on shared inputs and return a counterexample on mismatch. A reconstruction counts as grounded only when nothing is refuted and verified weight clears --min-information, so restating MZ and .text does not pass. The README reports a 19-DLL prologue-prior test where the textbook prior was wrong every time and the verifier accepted none of those errors.

Riyas's LB7UG prototype pairs an RP2040 with an Si5351 VFO and Si4732 receiver so WSJT-X or FT8CN sees one USB cable for UAC2 audio and Kenwood TS-480 CAT, with no extra sound card. Hubble tracks a new decagon around Saturn's south pole

Hubble OPAL images, published Wednesday in Science Advances, show a giant 10-sided atmospheric wave around Saturn's south pole — the first large regular-sided jet pattern seen in that hemisphere. Ground observers including Trevor Barry and Jean-Paul Oger flagged an undulating band in 2024; Hubble traces it back to 2023. Cassini (2004–2017) showed no long-lived southern counterpart to the 40-year northern hexagon, and the new wave sits in a jet through multiple altitudes. Amy Simon says it appears to be strengthening; whether it settles like the hexagon is still open.

LZ records one unexplained event in a dark-matter search

LUX-ZEPLIN, 10 tonnes of xenon a mile under SURF, found one particle interaction in 220 live days (March 2023–April 2024) that is hard to explain with known backgrounds. Spokesperson Rick Gaitskell is not claiming a dark-matter detection; the result is 2.6 sigma, about a 0.5% chance of a known-background fluctuation, far from 5 sigma. If it were a WIMP it would likely be at least 200 GeV/c² with a non-minimal interaction, in a higher-energy window than earlier LZ searches. The talk was at TeVPA 2026; a paper is headed to arXiv and Physical Review Letters, and more livetime is already on disk.

BepiColombo jettisons its Mercury Transfer Module

On 3 September 2026 ESA confirmed MTM separation from the BepiColombo stack more than 200 million kilometres from Earth. A Doppler shift at 13:41 BST was followed at 14:52 BST by Estrack Cebreros and Malargüe locks showing MPO solar arrays charging; telemetry is nominal. ESA's MPO now powers the composite until JAXA's Mio separates on 9–10 December, with Mercury orbit insertion planned for 21 November 2026 and science in April 2027. The cruise from the 20 October 2018 Ariane 5 launch ran about eight years.

Sci Amy Pavlak Laird / Carnegie Mellon University Science › Recommended

An in-plane Hall effect in a 2D heterostructure

Singh, Katoch, Chatterjee and colleagues at CMU report in Nature Materials an in-plane anomalous Hall signal in few-layer TaIrTe4 proximity-coupled to ferromagnetic Cr2Ge2Te6. The Hall effect has been treated as needing a field perpendicular to the film; here the same ultrathin device also responds to in-plane magnetization. The authors frame a possible single-chip vector magnetometer, but the CGT ferromagnetism is at low temperature and they are still checking room-temperature behaviour. The 28 August 2026 CMU note is the institutional account of doi:10.1038/s41563-026-02611-9.

Norway banned skateboards from 1978 to 1989

Wikipedia records a nationwide Norwegian ban on using, importing, selling, or advertising skateboards from 15 March 1978 until 8 May 1989, administered as product control by the Environment Ministry rather than as a street ordinance. Norway was the only country with an all-out ban; skaters built boards, smuggled parts, and hid ramps in forests that police still raided, with a slight loosening for organized skating from 1984. The statutory order FOR-1978-09-07-9022 is on Lovdata. The article treats it as risk-management bureaucracy and moral panic, not a traffic rule.

The Order of the Occult Hand

American newspaper writers have for decades smuggled the clause “It was as if some occult hand had…” into print as a membership badge. Joseph Flanders of The Charlotte News coined it in an August 1965 shooting story; colleagues formed an order to repeat it, and the phrase later appeared in the Times, the Globe, and elsewhere until James Janega's 2004 Chicago Tribune investigation. Paul Greenberg later wrote that a new secret phrase was chosen in 2006. Wikipedia is the synthesis; Flanders's original clip is linked from the page.
