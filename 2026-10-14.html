Redesigning source control at Anthropic

Anthropic says daily merges to its monorepo grew from about 2,500 in May to more than 30,000 in September, turning source control and review into infrastructure bottlenecks. It describes building Scion, an in-house version-control system, plus review and code-search tools, and migrating the monorepo from Git in five months.

Minions: A Dynamic and Distributed Virtual GPU Cloud Built on Idle Apple Silicon Devices

Apple Research presents Minions, a system that pools idle GPUs across Apple Silicon Macs into a distributed inference resource. The paper focuses on fault-tolerant task execution and using existing devices to soften centralized GPU capacity and cost constraints; it is a systems approach, not a claim that Macs replace dedicated clusters in every workload.

When Agents Talk, Secrets Travel: Topology-Driven Leakage and Recipient-Conditioned Sanitization in Multi-Agent LLM Systems

This paper studies how information disclosed to one authorized agent can propagate through ordinary coordination to an unauthorized agent, even without a malicious participant. Its evaluation spans coordinator-worker, peer-to-peer, and hierarchical topologies, and motivates defenses that account for who will receive a message rather than merely labeling the original data as sensitive.

QUAN-Transcribe-1.0: streaming Vietnamese speech recognition in 356M parameters

A 356M-parameter Vietnamese ASR model adapts the small EmbeddingGemma 2 audio encoder with a CTC reader, supporting both full-context and chunked streaming transcription. Its model card reports 6.87% average word error rate across its listed test sets and gives training data, inference code, and benchmark methodology; the speed result is measured on an H100, not a low-end device.

d1-3B: a small multimodal model for calibrated decisions

Liquid AI’s 3.12B-parameter model takes text, structured state, and images with named questions, then returns typed decisions in one forward pass rather than generating a chat response. The model card documents its 32K context, usage code, and benchmark claims, positioning it for routing, classification, and visual checks; quantized weights should fit the intended small-model class, though the vendor’s latency figures use high-end hardware.

ESP-SDR Can Now Transmit

A follow-on ESP32 radio experiment has moved from receiving raw 2.4 GHz samples to transmitting, extending recent work that exposed low-level radio access on several ESP32 variants. It is a striking example of how inexpensive embedded hardware can double as an experimental software-defined radio platform, with the usual need to respect local radio regulations.

2026 Retrocomputing Challenge: USBSID-Pico has Two SIDs, Many Inputs

The USBSID-Pico build combines a Raspberry Pi Pico with two SID sound chips and a broad set of inputs, creating a flexible USB-connected platform for reproducing the distinctive sound of classic Commodore machines. It is a practical retro-audio project where the hardware choices and interface design are as interesting as the nostalgia.

Turning a Wi-Fi 7 router into a wideband software-defined receiver

The w1700k-sdr project repurposes a Gemtek Wi-Fi 7 router’s MediaTek radio to capture raw 14-bit IQ data at up to 320 Msps, then streams it over 10 GbE to a Linux host for spectrum display. It turns otherwise closed networking hardware into an ambitious wideband receiver, with project code and the hardware pipeline documented for further exploration.

A six-port USB-to-UART bridge built on RP2040 or RP2350

PicoUart turns an RP2040 or RP2350 board into six independent USB serial ports, combining hardware UARTs with PIO-based channels and DMA-backed data paths. A custom RP2350B carrier board turns a Pi Pico 2 into a small computer for Varvara, the virtual machine behind UXN. It adds two PS/2 ports, a three-port USB hub, NES controller input, a real-time clock, and a second microSD slot—an unusually complete interface set for a microcontroller-based retro-computing build.

Picothermal: a USB-C thermal camera

Picothermal upgrades an earlier compact thermal-camera design from a 32×24 sensor to a 160×120 FLIR Lepton 3.5 and sends the image over USB-C to a phone instead of adding a screen and battery. The trade-off keeps the handheld build lean while substantially increasing thermal detail, and the maker has documented the project on their blog.

A 900-page pre-Pantone guide to color from 1692, now scanned in high resolution

Artist A. Boogert made a single copy of a Dutch color-mixing manual in 1692, with more than 700 pages devoted to color swatches and instructions. A high-resolution digital scan makes the 900-page work available to browse; it predates modern color standards, though there is little evidence that this lone copy influenced later color theory.

High-speed video reveals how honey bee stingers pump venom

High-speed imaging reveals that a honey bee’s barbed stinger is not simply a passive needle: moving components act as a paired pump to draw venom from the gland and deliver it into the target. The mechanism helps explain how the sting continues to inject after the bee has flown away, and offers a vivid example of biomechanics hiding in a familiar structure.

Rebuilding the Original Odhner Model 127 Pinwheel Calculator

John Wolff documents a detailed reconstruction of the Odhner Model 127, a mechanical pinwheel calculator whose arithmetic depends on carefully sequenced gears, sliding pins, and a clearing mechanism. The build makes the machine’s design and operation tangible through a hands-on engineering account rather than treating it as a museum curiosity.
