Energy-Based Reasoners: 99.9% on Sudoku-Extreme with 1.8M parameters

EBRs learn an energy function over latent reasoning states and treat each looped refinement step as gradient descent on that energy — the energy scores candidates without an external verifier, and its gradient doubles as a natural stopping signal. A 1.8M-parameter model hits 99.9% on Sudoku-Extreme using 345x less inference compute than the next-best approach, with adaptive stopping preserving accuracy at ~11x fewer FLOPs. A genuinely different architecture for algorithmic reasoning: small, self-verifying, and done looping when the landscape says so.

NarrowSpec: the draft model's hidden width need not match the target

Speculative-decoding drafters like DFlash and EAGLE-3 inherit the target model's hidden width by convention; this paper shows the draft's residual stream can run at half or a quarter width with largely unchanged acceptance rates. End-to-end decoding speeds up 1.13-1.44x on a DGX Spark's GB10 and up to 1.21x at large batches on B200, with no narrow draft ever slower than full width. Practical free lunch for local inference stacks — the trained output adapter even folds into the borrowed vocabulary projection.

Metrics-in-the-Loop: complexity feedback reshapes agent-written code

Coding agents guided only by passing tests let complexity and coverage gaps accumulate, then build on top of them. The authors inject cheap code metrics mid-trajectory — naming functions over a cyclomatic-complexity limit or lines skipped by tests — giving the agent a chance to refactor before continuing. Across three long-horizon benchmarks and four strong models, complexity feedback significantly reduces code erosion without hurting held-out correctness. A concrete, cheap recipe for agent 're-railing' that any agent harness could adopt today.

ThunderTalk ASR 0.6B: measured 3x faster dictation via honest 8-bit quantization

A 0.4B-parameter dictation model fine-tuned from Qwen3-ASR-0.6B for Chinese, English, and code-switched speech, with the text decoder quantized to 8-bit (1.2 GB total) and the audio encoder kept in fp16. The card shows exactly how it earns its keep: token error rate drops from 8.7% to 8.0% overall on a 44-recording human-referenced set, and decoding time falls from 36 ms to 12 ms per token — because dictation is memory-bound, halving weight bytes nearly triples throughput. Runs on Apple Silicon via MLX; the honest per-domain table (including where it regresses slightly) is a model card done right.

Peking University's KCL released a 2.21B decision model on Qwen3.5-2B that answers typed questions — choice (up to 255 options), yes/no, and ordered score — with a probability per option in a single forward pass, generating no text. ch32fun: an open-source minimal stack for WCH CH32/CH5xx RISC-V MCUs

The CH32 RISC-V User Group published ch32fun, a no-HAL development environment for the 10-cent CH32V003 (48 MHz RISC-V, 16 kB flash, 2 kB RAM) and the wider CH32/CH5xx family: headers, examples, and minichlink flashing plus gdbserver, built with plain gcc-riscv64 on Windows, Linux, or WSL. Astronomers watched two stars in Andromeda merge — 'almost like a bomb exploding inside a living star'

For the first time, a team tracked a binary star merger in real time: a ~0.7-solar-mass companion spiraled into a 7-solar-mass supergiant nicknamed Big Bao at the edge of Andromeda, unifying after just seven orbits (~seven years) of common-envelope drag. NEOWISE flagged the anomaly in 2023, SPHEREx caught the July 2025 dimming, ZTF saw the October 2025 eruption peak within a week, and Webb observed just 47 days later — the earliest follow-up of such an event ever — letting the team measure common-envelope efficiency directly instead of reverse-engineering ancient events. The Nature paper lands as Roman's time-domain surveys promise many more.

125,000 years ago hippos roamed the Arabian Desert — and early humans hunted them, possibly with poisoned weapons

Excavations in southern Saudi Arabia from the 'Green Arabia' interglacial uncovered thousands of stone tools beside hippo and other megafauna fossils from ~125,000 years ago, when the desert was a lake-and-grassland ecosystem. Rare preserved plant remains hint early humans processed vegetation and may have coated weapons with poisonous plant compounds — potentially among the earliest evidence of poison hunting. A vivid snapshot of a completely vanished climate right where oil fields sit today.

Electron pairs that survive at room temperature sharpen the puzzle of room-temperature superconductivity

Superconductivity needs two ingredients: electrons pairing up, and those pairs moving in lockstep without resistance. Utrecht researchers found the first ingredient — stable electron pairs — can exist at far higher temperatures than theory allowed, meaning the barrier to lossless electricity at room temperature may be the pairing's coordination, not its formation. It is not a room-temperature superconductor; it is more useful than that — it re-points where the fix has to come from after a century of chasing.

More than a billion years of Earth's missing history may have been eroded by continents bobbing on mantle currents

Earth's rock record contains notorious 'great unconformities' where hundreds of millions to over a billion years of strata are simply absent, and no one has agreed why. New work led by the University of Wollongong proposes deep-mantle currents slowly raised and lowered entire continents, letting erosion strip vast chapters of crust away before the remaining layers were buried and preserved. If it holds, plate tectonics' deep engine becomes the reason our planet's own diary has burned pages.

The barreleye fish — transparent head, rotating tubular eyes — captured in 4K at 600 meters

MBARI researchers filmed an extraordinarily rare barreleye (Macropinna microstoma) nearly 2,000 feet down, in sharp 4K, watching it scan upward through its glass-clear head dome with tubular green eyes, then rotate those eyes forward to track chosen prey. The transparent-head strategy — a protective dome over eyes that live in two orientations at once — had mostly been seen in dead specimens; this is behavior few humans have ever watched. The footage is the kind that makes deep-sea biology worth funding.
