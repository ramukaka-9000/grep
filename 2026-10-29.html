The asteroid that killed the dinosaurs may have helped ants conquer the world

A large genomic study finds bursts of 'jumping gene' activity in ant genomes coincided with the rise of the major ant lineages right after the K-Pg extinction, possibly sharpening their chemoreception and letting them exploit the emptied ecosystems. In Colonial Williamsburg, thieving rats saved history

Archaeologists dismantling a wall at the Bray School site in Colonial Williamsburg found a centuries-old rat's nest that rodents had turned into a time capsule: a silver fork, seeds, textiles and other everyday objects preserved in the cavity, many of them missing from the normal archaeological record. It is a lovely inversion - the vermin that nibbled colonists' belongings accidentally curated a snapshot of 18th-century domestic life. Nest archaeology has found similar caches in European cathedrals, which makes the Williamsburg find part of a weirdly systematic field.

Two of Jupiter's moons just occulted each other

Yesterday's APOD captures a genuinely rare geometry: from Earth, Ganymede passed directly in front of Io, a mutual occultation that happens only when the alignment of Jupiter's system with our line of sight briefly permits it. The frames show Io vanishing behind the larger moon - a chance to watch two worlds a few hundred million kilometers away line up to pixel accuracy. Mutual occultations like this are not just pretty: historically, timing them was how astronomers refined the Galilean moons' orbits and chased the anomalies that led to Io's volcanism being predicted.
