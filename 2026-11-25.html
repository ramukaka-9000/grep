GPT-6 models demonstrate intrinsic protein-design capabilities

OpenAI reports that its GPT-6 models can propose protein structures and sequences using general reasoning and code rather than calling specialist design models. In lab tests, proteins based on GPT-6.2 designs showed binding evidence across five targets, though specialist tools still helped with candidate selection and the results are a company-reported preprint rather than an independent evaluation.

Learning Compact State Abstractions for LLM Agents

Instead of repeatedly passing an agent's entire interaction history, the authors learn inspectable recursive programs that retain only information needed to preserve task performance. On ALFWorld and ScienceWorld, the learned state cuts mean policy input by up to 71% and 57%, respectively, while keeping success near a reference agent.

No Fixed Configuration Is Best: Context- and Cost-Aware Tuning of LLM Pipelines

A study of schema-matching pipelines finds that the right structural choice can matter far more than numerical prompt or retrieval tuning: a one-table restriction shifts accuracy in opposite directions on different tasks. Running both alternatives and using a judge only when they disagree reached 91.5% top-1 accuracy on 41 tasks, at about one cent per column; that result costs 5.9 times the default configuration.

Indra OCR: CPU document parsing in a 1.25M-parameter model

This compact OCR package targets CPU use and combines script-specific readers with layout, tables, and inline LaTeX handling. Its model card reports 78.9 on OmniParseBench across 2,937 pages and 16,288 tests; it also warns that handwriting, complex tables, and degraded scans remain difficult.

Pico-WWVB: a Raspberry Pi Pico time transmitter for radio clocks

A Pico 2 W fetches time over Wi-Fi, encodes the WWVB format, and uses its PIO to generate a 60 kHz signal for nearby radio-controlled clocks that cannot receive the distant broadcast indoors. The MicroPython code and simple loop-antenna build are public; the demonstrated range is only inches, making this an instructive low-power experiment rather than a replacement transmitter.

TNY-360: an open-hardware ESP32-S3 quadruped with closed-loop control

This buildable robot dog combines an ESP32-S3, 12 modified hobby servos, custom modular boards, and a 200 Hz feedback control loop. Its public repository includes firmware, CAD, PCB files, and calibration features that help diagnose assembly errors, making the design unusually useful as a repairable robotics learning platform.

SecurePair: encrypted pairing between two ESP32 boards, confirmed with an LED

SecurePair demonstrates an end-to-end encrypted link between ESP32 boards using only a button and LED on each device for user-confirmed key establishment. The beta library handles fresh-key agreement and authenticated messages, offering a concrete embedded example of pairing without a screen or keyboard.

NASA’s Titan-bound Dragonfly rotorcraft gets its eyes

NASA has integrated DragonCam's seven science cameras into the Dragonfly rotorcraft, which is scheduled to launch in 2028 and reach Titan in 2034. The cameras will map terrain from the air and surface, while ultraviolet-capable illumination may help reveal fluorescent organic molecules during Titan's long night.

A tiny spider named for Stephen Colbert has a genome 50% larger than a human’s

The Californian trapdoor spider Aptostichus stephencolberti has a 4.6-billion-base-pair genome, about 50% larger than a human genome, with 77% made up of repetitive sequences. The newly published reference genome also records 52 chromosomes and may help conservation work on a species confined to vulnerable coastal dunes.
