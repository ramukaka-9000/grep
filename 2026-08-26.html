The paper uncovers ELR collapse in LM pretraining: loss trajectories collapse onto each other when the ratio of learning rate to parameter norm (the effective learning rate) is matched across runs. A genuinely useful result for anyone training or scaling language models.

Three forgotten kings could rewrite the history of ancient Assyria

A new study in the Journal of Cuneiform Studies argues the canonical Assyrian King List quietly erased at least three rulers: a rival Tiglath-pileser who seized power after a 763 BCE solar eclipse, a Shalmaneser who preceded Tiglath-pileser III, and an Aššur-uballiṭ who reigned around 913-912 BCE. The evidence comes from a British Museum clay tablet, an 1894 stela, and a damaged statue. The authors suggest the King List was less an impartial record than a legitimizing canon that periodically removed short-lived or parallel rulers.

Scientists just imaged the hidden quantum shape of a molecule

A Göttingen team has produced the first complete 3D image of an organic molecule's wavefunction — the quantum object that governs how molecules absorb light and react — using lab-based photoelectron spectroscopy plus a redesigned reconstruction algorithm. The new approach needs far less data and no synchrotron, and the authors say it could lead to femtosecond-resolution 3D 'movies' of molecules changing state. Wavefunctions remain unmeasurable directly, but this makes their shape practical to visualize.

Sci ScienceDaily / NASA Goddard Science › Recommended

NASA finds Earth microbes could survive on the Moon

A NASA-led study in Science Advances modeled conditions at three lunar South Pole sites and found that hardy Earth microbes — including the common fungus Aspergillus niger — could survive for at least a day in cold, shadowed niches as small as a boot print. Astronaut contamination could muddy future searches for pristine lunar chemistry, and the same logic applies to Mars sample return. The team argues the Moon could double as a natural lab for testing microbial survival limits.

Forces deep inside Earth helped Antarctica freeze before the Arctic

New modeling in Science suggests slow-moving mantle waves — triggered when Africa and Antarctica rifted apart — lifted East Antarctica's plateau and Gamburtsev Mountains above ~2 km, letting ice take hold around 34 million years ago even while the planet was ~5°C warmer. Arctic landmasses stayed lower and didn't glaciate for another 30 million years, explaining the long-standing asymmetry between the poles. The work recasts deep-Earth geology as a preconditioner for ice ages.

Newton's 300-year-old law just passed its biggest test yet

Using Atacama Cosmology Telescope observations of hundreds of thousands of galaxy clusters, a team led by Penn's Patricio Gallardo tested gravity on the largest scale yet — and found the inverse-square law holding almost exactly as Newton and Einstein predicted. The result constrains modified-gravity theories like MOND and strengthens the case that dark matter, not altered physics, explains the missing mass. Published in Physical Review Letters.

A hidden "on switch" in human DNA has finally been decoded

UCSD researchers measured gene expression across ~500,000 engineered versions of the 'initiator' DNA element, then trained machine-learning models that decoded its sequence signature — and found roughly 60% of human genes contain it. The models can predict how initiator mutations affect gene activity, which could aid synthetic promoter design and disease research. A concrete case of AI decoding the human gene-expression code.
