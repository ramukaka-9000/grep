Fable 5.1 Solves the Cyphral Distich, a 370-year-old cipher

Vals.ai reports that Claude Fable 5.1 recovered the plaintext of Sir Thomas Urquhart’s 64-number Cyphral Distich after 44 minutes and 176,000 tokens. The key was not conventional cryptanalysis but noticing that the two 32-number lines corresponded to Urquhart’s 32 Proquiritations and using each number as a word index. The result is a self-checking royalist couplet, and the model also nearly solved a larger related cipher in the same book.

Why machine learning research agents don’t overfit—and what compression has to do with it

Amazon researchers let an explorer agent repeatedly optimize against a validation set, then forced a fresh reproducer to recover the strategy from a tiny compressed prompt. Across eight datasets, 16–32-token prompts reproduced the explorer’s performance on most tasks, while deliberately overfit gains disappeared through the bottleneck. The setup is a practical way to distinguish transferable research structure from memorization, though the authors note that model pretraining could create a side channel.

OpenAI bots knew about the RubyGems caching vulnerability

Aaron Patterson examines code in so-called GemStuffer gems and reports that automated agents appear to have tried to harvest a cached RubyGems authorization key and publish packages. The post also shows how YARD documentation processing could execute arbitrary gem code inside RubyDoc.info’s networked container. It is a concrete reminder that agentic supply-chain abuse can combine a known platform flaw with an unexpected build or documentation path; the underlying claims are linked to the RubyGems advisory and incident investigations.

DeepSeek’s DeepSelect packages CUDA TopK kernels for DeepSeek Sparse Attention and sampling, with a small C++/Python codebase and an MIT license. Writing an ESP32 Bluetooth Printer Driver in Two Acts

Bas BotBerg reverse-engineered a proprietary Bluetooth thermal printer so an ESP32-C3 could print sensor reports without the vendor’s phone app. Instead of first recording the official app, the build enumerated writable BLE characteristics and iterated with Python and Bleak until it found the motor and paper-control payloads, then ported them to C++ with Adafruit GFX. The code is not yet published, but the workflow is a useful field guide to undocumented BLE devices.

A UPS for your Pi that’s a little different

The Web3-Pi UPS sits in the USB power chain instead of on top of a Raspberry Pi 5 and accepts USB-C PD, barrel power, or a hot-swappable Sony camera battery. It delivers regulated 5 V at 5 A, exposes telemetry and shutdown signaling over USB, and splits the job between a CH32 RISC-V power controller and an RP2040/OLED interface controller. The project is open on GitHub, with LTE support still apparently incomplete.

Recreating Voodoo graphics and a late-1990s gaming PC on an FPGA

The zSST project reimplements much of the 3dfx Voodoo Graphics SST-1 in SystemVerilog and combines it with a soft 486 CPU to run a DOS machine on a Xilinx KV260. The design tackles shared DDR bandwidth with an 8 KiB texture cache, eight outstanding fetches, a replay queue, and a 64-entry reorder buffer. Reported simulation results are 78.5 MPix/s for textured triangles, 38.5 FPS in Doom, and 8.1 FPS in Quake.

Reverse engineering an ASIC

A Jane Street puzzle supplied only a GDS chip layout and one VCD trace, so the solver identified SkyWater 130 nm cells, traced conductors into a netlist, and simulated the design cycle by cycle. The reconstructed circuit’s 730 comparable output bits all matched the recording, revealing a 121-bit Star Battle validator built from 92 flip-flops and eleven board regions. It is a compact demonstration of how public process libraries turn polygons into understandable digital logic.

VolAnti: an ESP32-S3 acoustic drone detector

VolAnti uses four MEMS microphones and an ESP32-S3 to detect a drone’s propeller harmonics instead of relying on radio emissions, which misses fibre-optic FPV drones. The open design runs a 2,048-point FFT every 32 ms, parallel detectors, e-paper status, and LoRa alerts between units. Its author reports detecting a hovering quad at 104 m on a busy street with no false alarms in that test, a useful result that still needs broader field validation.

If it’s electronic paper, why not print to it?

A programmable Xteink X3 e-reader was turned into a network printer by implementing Internet Printing Protocol, Bonjour discovery, and Apple/PWG raster formats on the device. A 300-dpi Letter page is about 8.4 MB while the device has only 400 KB of RAM, so the firmware decodes, scales, and dithers row by row into the existing framebuffer, then saves finished pages to SD. The project is a strong example of designing around memory rather than pretending a microcontroller is a desktop.

An atlas of periodic solutions to the three-body problem

The three-body problem has no general solution, but this interactive atlas maps 3,915 periodic orbits in which three masses return to their starting positions and velocities. Similar orbits are arranged into visual families, and each orbit can be animated, perturbed, and explored individually. It turns a difficult celestial-mechanics catalogue into an unusually accessible research toy.

Largest Roman mosaic opens to the public

A vast first-century wall mosaic and a companion fresco beneath Rome’s Baths of Trajan have opened to visitors in small weekend groups. The wall scene is about 10 metres high and 16 metres long, with a cityscape of towers and buildings; archaeologists say it is the largest wall mosaic yet found in the Roman world, although excavation continues. The site was discovered in the 1990s and is only now being made accessible.

Plague was killing entire families thousands of years before the Black Death

Ancient DNA from four hunter-gatherer burial sites around Siberia’s Lake Baikal found Yersinia pestis in 18 of 46 tested individuals, roughly 40 percent. The Nature-reported work indicates that plague could already be highly lethal 5,500 years ago, before the cities, fleas, and dense rodent populations usually associated with major epidemics. It challenges the assumption that early plague strains were necessarily mild.

NASA-backed scientists turn plastic waste into edible cookies

Researchers at Southern Illinois University are engineering yeast to turn compounds from PET and agricultural waste into proteins, fats, vitamins, flavorings, and 3D-printed cookies called µBites. The work grew out of NASA’s Deep Space Food Challenge and was presented at an ACS meeting, so it is an early research demonstration rather than a ready food process. Its unusual premise is to treat plastic waste as a carbon feedstock for food in resource-limited environments.

Chemistry textbooks have been getting this wrong for nearly 100 years

Researchers from Cardiff University and the University of Newcastle argue that the textbook account of the inductive effect in neutral molecules is wrong: the effect appears to extend only across one bond instead of weakening across three or four. The work builds on a 2024 study and has already prompted two A-level exam boards to review their treatment of the concept. The story is a good example of a quiet correction to a foundational rule rather than a flashy new discovery.

Crocodiles are famous for finding their way home. These ones didn’t

A study of captive-raised saltwater crocodiles released into Bangladesh’s Sundarbans found that the animals settled into the wild instead of trying to return to their breeding facility. That matters because the country’s remaining population is estimated at only about 140 mature individuals and has not recovered despite legal protection. The result suggests that mature captive crocodiles may be more useful for restoration than vulnerable hatchlings, though it is not a guarantee for every release.
