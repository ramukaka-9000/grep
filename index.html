TextSeal2: distortion-free LLM watermarking that survives speculative decoding

Meta's TextSeal2 is a distortion-free watermark whose signal survives speculative decoding — tokens replacing rejected drafts stay watermarked under the same keys, with unchanged acceptance rate, preserved output diversity, and no measurable latency. It merges multiple keys into one virtual key via a Gaussian copula and uses a memoryless exponential race to reproduce block verification exactly. Open code at facebookresearch/textseal; the practical watermarking paper for anyone shipping production LLM text.

75,000-year-old Neanderthal may have been killed by a spear aimed at the heart

A small circular hole in the shoulder blade of the Shanidar A Neanderthal from Iraqi Kurdistan suggests a sharpened wooden spear was driven through the chest — potentially the oldest evidence in the hominin fossil record of fatal interpersonal violence with a wooden weapon. WB The MTE Letter Web ›Discuss › Recommended

Zinc creep in cable spelter sockets brought down the Arecibo telescope

The National Academies' final report pins the 2020 Arecibo platform collapse on long-term zinc creep in the cable spelter sockets — the first documented case of its kind despite a century of zinc-socket use. The failure sequence began with Hurricane Maria more than three years earlier, with minimal warning signs before cables pulled out. A sobering reminder that unique science infrastructure needs inspection regimes, not just maintenance budgets.

WB Ars Technica Web ›Discuss › Recommended

Scientists recreate the 1,600-year-old recipe of the Lycurgus Cup

University of Sheffield scientists cracked the recipe behind the ~400 CE Lycurgus Cup's dichroic glass — green in front light, deep red in backlit glow — a nanotechnology effect from gold-silver alloy nanoparticles that Roman glassmakers achieved empirically. They produced a contemporary sculpture reproducing the exact optical effect, blending the Lycurgus story with the Farnese Atlas, globe swapped for a cup. It goes on public display at the Millennium Gallery during Sheffield's Festival of the Mind before a permanent home in the Turner Museum of Glass.

WB Brookhaven National Laboratory Web › Recommended

Scientists smash oxygen nuclei to recreate matter from the Big Bang

At RHIC, near-light-speed oxygen-nucleus collisions produced quark-gluon plasma — the primordial soup of the early universe — in a much smaller, lower-energy system than previously demonstrated. The jet-quenching signatures show the exotic state forms well below the heavy-ion regimes where textbooks said it needed to, redrawing the boundary of early-universe physics. It also opens lighter collision systems as precision probes of the plasma.
