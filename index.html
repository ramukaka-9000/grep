RepRapMicron: a RepRap that prints at ~1 micron

Vik Olliver's µRepRap aims to do for micron-scale fabrication what RepRap did for 3D printing: the current PIKA build achieves repeatable positioning around 1µm over an 8mm cube using off-the-shelf NEMA17 steppers, 3D-printed flexure stages descended from the OpenFlexure microscope, a hypodermic-needle probe, and UV nail-gel resin deposited in sub-15µm droplets — it even etches its own probes. MMX: Mars moon lander launches tonight for the first Phobos sample return

JAXA's MMX mission lifts off on an H3 from Tanegashima at 04:41 JST on 20 October — 01:11 IST tonight — on the first attempt ever to land on a moon other than ours and bring samples home. It will orbit Phobos, deploy the 25-kg Idefix rover (which 'weighs' about 15 grams in Phobos's gravity), land, and return at least 10 grams of regolith to Australia in 2031. Phobos's origin — captured asteroid or giant-impact debris — is genuinely unresolved, and these samples decide the question. Watch live on JAXA's YouTube channel.

Physicists create a new quantum state: the fractional Fermi sea

By cycling ultracold 1D cesium atoms between strong attraction and strong repulsion, Innsbruck theorists showed the gas reorganizes into a highly excited state that keeps hidden order — a 'fractional Fermi sea' whose Friedel oscillations and correlation decay don't fit the long-established Tomonaga-Luttinger-liquid textbook. The theory paper is out in Physical Review Letters with the experimental realization in peer review. Possible new quasiparticles are on the table; the team is floating the name 'super-Fermions'.

Two monster black holes at the center of Mrk 501 could collide within 100 years

Twenty-three years of multi-frequency radio imaging of galaxy Mrk 501 revealed a second, previously unseen jet sweeping counterclockwise around the known one — what the team calls first direct imaging evidence of a close supermassive black-hole binary, orbiting every 121 days at just 250–540 AU separation. Depending on their 100-million-to-billion solar masses, they could merge within a human century, which is nothing astronomically. Pulsar timing arrays may catch the gravitational-wave frequency rising before the show, making this the rare merger with a countdown.

A 1,200-year-old mummified hairless dog found in Peru — identified by its missing teeth

At the Wari Empire site Castillo de Huarmey, archaeologists found a naturally mummified dog — hairless skin and ears intact — that radiocarbon-dates to at least 1,200 years, the site's oldest specimen. The clincher for identifying it as a Peruvian hairless dog: missing first premolars, a trait caused by the same gene as hairlessness. Isotope analysis showed hairless puppies were fed like human children, hinting at organized breeding 400 years before the Inca; the study is in the Journal of Anthropological Archaeology.

Japan's 7,300-year-old volcanic apocalypse had a twist: some populations thrived

The Kikai-Akahoya eruption buried Japan under meters of ash and is usually told as an archaeology of annihilation. A Lund-led demographic reconstruction (Science Advances) shows neither textbook story holds: southern Kyushu had already emptied, northern Kyushu kept growing through the catastrophe as refugees arrived and pivoted to marine resources — while populations far from the volcano unexpectedly stalled, possibly because ash-damaged forage wore down the teeth of deer and boar, the Jomon's food supply. Catastrophe outcomes turned out to be choices, not verdicts.
