Anthropic previews the Model Hardware Standard for agents in the lab

Anthropic opened a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard, a shared driver so AI agents can discover and operate lab and factory instruments — microscopes, liquid handlers, robot arms — through MCP, CLI, or APIs. It started as a collaboration with HHMI Janelia to collapse weeks of bespoke integrations; early partners include AWS Strands Robots, Universal Robots, Tecan, Raspberry Pi, and Hugging Face LeRobot. The spec is model-agnostic and not yet open source: the preview exists to build physical-safety evaluations first. Claude still needs expert oversight on spatial failures, which the announcement is explicit about.

Small Models Have Arrived

Calvin French-Owen argues the interesting story is not another frontier model but cheap, fast ones: gpt-5.6-luna ripping through code and email at ~100 tps for tens of cents, with GLM 5.3 sitting on a new Pareto frontier. Ax He et al. / arXiv arXiv › Recommended

When Context Gets Root: privilege escalation in LLM harnesses

This paper names instruction privilege escalation: agent harnesses assemble context in ways that can promote low-privilege content into a higher instruction tier, so the model follows attacker text it would have ignored at the original level. Across 13 attack objectives on six coding-agent harnesses, unrestricted execution hit every objective on every harness, and automatic permission review did not save the three harnesses that offer it. The authors also reproduce the issue with harness-provided persistent goals and scheduled tasks.

WeMM-Embedding — Tencent's open multimodal embedding family

Tencent's WeChat Vision team open-sourced WeMM-Embedding, universal multimodal embedding models at 2B, 4B, and 9B that handle text, images, video, visual documents, and interleaved inputs, with Matryoshka dimensions down to 64. Experiential — open gateway that trains a router from your traffic

Experiential is an open-source OpenAI-compatible gateway that sits across hosted, BYOK, and local models, then turns production traces into a fitted router or a fine-tuned model. spark-13m-instruct is a 13.2M-parameter Llama-style English decoder — hidden 384, 6 layers, GQA, 1024 context — instruction-tuned on 200k SmolTalk conversations after ~17B-token pretraining. Nawah-Math-Reasoning: 52M Arabic CoT math that runs on CPU

Nawah-Math-Reasoning is a 51.8M-parameter Arabic chain-of-thought math model that writes a `<think>` trace then an answer, small enough for CPU. The card reports 79.0% number-agreement on a pinned GSM8K-ar split and a +18.2 point jump to 52.2% on synthetic relational problems that previous mixes failed. The author is explicit that GSM8K-ar is machine-translated from few templates, synthetic rows are arithmetic-checked not sense-checked, and this is a proof of concept — score numbers, not strings.

Running multiple Linux kernels without a hypervisor

Hackaday covers Cong Wang's mklinux v7.0-mk2: multiple independent Linux kernels on bare metal, no hypervisor. A host kernel carves CPUs, memory, and PCI devices and boots spawn kernels via kexec_file_load(); LWN and the multikernel.io guide describe native performance versus KVM plus kerf, Lazy CMA, and DAXFS. Isolation is a separate kernel, not a VM. The name collides with 1990s MkLinux, which this is not.

3D 3D Printing Industry / EPFL 3D Print › Recommended

EPFL powers micro-robots with sound, not motors

EPFL researchers (Hwang et al., Science Advances) 3D-print Helmholtz resonators that turn a sound field into a directional air jet, so micro-robots can produce thrust without an onboard motor. Centimeter boats with three differently tuned resonators traced an infinity path (5.7 mm mean error); a 184-microgram 2PP flyer with Gurney-flap rotors hovered near 6,500 rpm under an ultrasonic phased array. Thrust scaled about 44 µN per extra cubic centimeter of cavity. Airborne designs still need a focused external sound field; that is the honest limit.

BepiColombo's Mercury arrival begins 3 September

On 3 September 2026, ESA's Mercury Transfer Module will separate from BepiColombo's two science orbiters — ESA's MPO and JAXA's Mio — after eight years stacked together, the first step of Mercury arrival. ESA will livestream from ESOC from 13:45 CEST, with separation around 14:00 and earliest signal acquisition about 15:53 CEST. The pair enter Mercury orbit in November and split in December; science starts April 2027. It is Europe and Japan's first Mercury orbiter mission, and only the second ever to orbit the planet.

VLT/SPHERE images a candidate companion to Betelgeuse

ScienceDaily reports Miguel Montargès and colleagues used ESO's VLT/SPHERE to image a candidate companion, Betelgeuse B, near greatest apparent separation in December 2024, with a paper to appear in Astronomy & Astrophysics. The companion looks more massive than the earlier ~1 solar-mass prediction — about two to three solar masses — which is why they could see it. Wikipedia independently lists a 2026 A&A note on VLT/SPHERE imaging of the candidate. The team says one more epoch a year later is still needed before the case is closed.

A 324-million-year-old stem insect still had swimming paddles

A Nature paper (26 August) led by Chenyang Cai (NIGPAS) and Erik Tihelka (Cambridge) describes Chosha praecursor, a ~324-million-year-old stem insect from Texas's Tesnus Formation, with paddle-like abdominal appendages no living insect has. Together with re-studied Devonian and Mazon Creek fossils, the authors argue insects spent a long amphibious phase rather than jumping straight onto land, helping fill an ~80-million-year hexapod gap. Wikipedia's insect-evolution page now cites the 2026 Chosha discovery from the Late Mississippian Tesnus Formation.

Rare, widespread snow reaches the Atacama's Pacific edge

NASA Earth Observatory documents rare, widespread August 2026 snowfall across Chile's Atacama, from the Andes to near the Pacific south of Antofagasta, in Landsat 8/9 and Terra MODIS imagery. ALMA on the Chajnantor plateau went into survival mode; Taltal saw ~40 mm of rain in three days, about ten times its annual mean, and SENAPRED reported flooding damage. Atmospheric scientist René Garreaud ties the cutoff low to a huge trough plus a strengthening El Niño. Snow in the Atacama is not unheard of (2011, 2025), but this one's coastal reach is the story.

Sci ScienceDaily / Nature, Parkes, IXPE Science › Recommended

A magnetar may show empty space twisting light

A Nature study led by Rachael E. Stewart, as summarized by ScienceDaily, reports radio (Parkes/Murriyang) plus IXPE and NICER X-ray polarimetry of magnetar 1E 1547.0-5408 that the authors interpret as possible vacuum birefringence — Heisenberg's 1930s prediction that a huge magnetic field can make empty space rotate the polarization of light. The object's nearly aligned magnetic and spin axes, viewed nearly pole-on, are unusually favorable. The same write-up is clear that confirmation needs more observations and simulations; this is a candidate detection, not a closed case.

NASA's Starling navigated without GPS using other satellites as landmarks

NASA's Starling mission flew FALCON, joint work with Stanford spinout EraDrive, using star-tracker cameras to identify other satellites and debris against a public catalog and then fix Starling's own orbit without GPS. In three days it also tightened the known orbits of more than 200 objects onboard. ScienceDaily quotes Ames program manager Roger Hunter; later this year the four Starling craft are slated to share tracks with one another.
