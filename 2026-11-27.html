How Much Do Coding Agents Over-Edit, and Why? Delta Residue Attribution on 7,660 agent trajectories

The authors introduce Delta Residue Attribution: replay agent trajectories, trace every patch block to the step that wrote it, revert it, and rerun the task tests to decide if the edit was needed. Applied to 7,660 resolved Copilot CLI trajectories across 18 frontier models on SWE-bench Verified and DeepSWE, the study quantifies which of the extra lines agents add are actually necessary. Directly relevant to anyone reviewing AI-generated patches.

Ax Griffin et al. (Oxford, OpenAI) — arXiv 2611.34934 arXiv › Recommended

Attack Policy Selection can Mislead Monitor Evaluators

Evaluations of AI monitors — classifiers that flag dangerous agent behaviour — depend heavily on how the proxy attack policies were generated. Using an ex-production OpenAI monitor, measured false-negative rates ranged from 0.5% to 20.8% across optimisation levels of a single RL method, and 6.4% to 23.0% across methods. A caution for anyone building or trusting AI-control evaluations: the metric you get is a function of the adversary you simulated.

Earth's next ice age may have already begun — global warming stopped it

Cosmogenic dating and ancient plants preserved beneath Barnes Ice Cap — the last remnant of the Laurentide Ice Sheet — show the ice began re-expanding about 2,000 years ago, marking the onset of the next glaciation, and reached maximum extent around 1960 before anthropogenic warming reversed it. The Science paper (Vol 394) attributes the termination of the next ice age directly to greenhouse forcing despite declining summer insolation. A cleanly verified, striking reframing of the Holocene's end.

This tiny spider named after Stephen Colbert has a genome 50% larger than a human's

The first reference genome for the trapdoor spider Aptostichus stephencolberti (yes, named after the comedian, at his own request in 2008) comes in at 4.6 billion base pairs across 52 chromosomes — versus 3.1 billion and 23 in humans — with 77% of it repetitive sequence. The Journal of Heredity assembly also finds all spiders lack telomere repeats, an ancestral loss that may explain spiders' fast chromosome evolution. A 345-million-year-old fossil had scientists fooled for decades

Synchrotron X-ray imaging at the European Synchrotron Radiation Facility revealed that 'Lizzie', long billed as the earliest reptile, actually has internal bony gills and a fish-like mouth packed with thousands of tiny teeth — a primitive aquatic or amphibious creature, not a reptile. The reclassification implies several land-adaptation traits evolved in animals still tied to water. Confirmed by the American Museum of Natural History's write-up.

Sci ScienceDaily / Northwestern University — Physical Review Letters Science › Recommended

Physicists trap a single antimatter particle for months in a wall-free cold trap

Northwestern's Gabrielse group captures positrons from sodium-22 decay, slows them through cold tungsten, and holds one in a wall-free electromagnetic trap at ~300 millikelvin — its quantum ground state — for months at a time. The 'one-positron quantum cyclotron' sets up the most precise electron/positron comparison yet, where any difference would crack the Standard Model. Note the underlying PRL result was published October 21; ScienceDaily re-ran it this week.

Scientists discover mysterious lakes more than a mile beneath the Red Sea

Sam Purkis's team and OceanX found the first documented brine pools in the Gulf of Aqaba at 1,770 meters — salt-saturated, oxygen-free lakes teeming with microbial life, found in the last five minutes of a ten-hour ROV dive. The pools preserve archives of ancient tsunamis and earthquakes, and their extremophile communities inform the search for life elsewhere.
