Re-creating NASA’s Heat Shield Problem

A DIY test recreates Orion-style segmented ablative heat-shield samples from NASA’s published material descriptions, cures them with thermocouples, and subjects them to propane-torch heating. The observed gas buildup and cracking track NASA’s explanation for the Artemis I heat-shield damage, while the author also notes experimental limitations instead of presenting the setup as a flight qualification test.

Analyzing the FScale Instruction in Intel’s 8087 FPU

Ken Shirriff’s continuing 8087 reverse engineering finds that FSCALE, which sounds like a simple power-of-two operation, expands into more than 140 micro-instructions and three levels of subroutine calls. The annotated die and microcode breakdown make the hidden cost of compatibility, exceptions, stack handling, and numerical edge cases visible inside an early PC floating-point unit.

New simulations of the Moon-forming impact include the temperature-dependent strength of early Earth and Theia instead of treating both bodies as fluids. Some runs produce an intact Moon in about five hours, challenging the familiar picture in which a debris disk must slowly assemble the Moon.

Griffith researchers link unusual rounded tooth grooves and biochemical evidence to repeated betel-nut use by foragers in Sulawesi as far back as 25,000–16,000 years ago. Physicists discover a hidden “curveball” in quantum light

A trapped calcium ion revealed the optical Magnus effect: in a tightly focused laser beam, the strongest interaction occurs slightly off the beam center, analogous to a spinning ball curving in flight. The measured shift is only a few hundred nanometers, but it could matter for laser-controlled qubits and may offer a way to couple them.
