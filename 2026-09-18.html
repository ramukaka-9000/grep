How GLM built its own inference infrastructure

Z.ai describes building a production inference service for GLM-5.3-Flash from scratch on a cluster of more than 100,000 Chinese-made accelerators, supporting a 1M-token context window and multimodal requests. The company says an Infra Agent powered by GLM-5.3 helped drive the work, while layered correctness tests, traces, microbenchmarks, and end-to-end measurements supplied the dense feedback needed to diagnose regressions. Z.ai reports that the resulting stack tripled end-to-end serving performance in under two weeks; those numbers are a vendor account, but the feedback-loop design is the part worth studying.

Bonsai 2 27B: near-lossless compression in a 9× smaller footprint

PrismML’s Ternary Bonsai 2 27B uses {-1, 0, +1} weights with FP16 group scaling, a 5.9GB footprint, and a 262K-token context window while accepting text-and-image input. The company reports 98.2% of the aggregate benchmark score of full-precision Qwen3.8 27B, with 143 tokens/s on an RTX 5090 and 46.8 tokens/s on an M5 Max. The release is a useful concrete test of whether low-bit deployment can preserve the coding, vision, and agentic behavior that normally disappears first in aggressive compression.

HN Wilhelm Klop / Detail HN ›Discuss › Must-Read

Towards Self-Driving Codebases

This essay argues that the next useful phase of coding agents is not more token-spending loops, but infrastructure that can detect and fix obvious bugs, debug production failures, enforce frontend consistency, and run bounded growth experiments. The human contribution shifts upward toward product ideas, architecture, and deciding which constraints matter, while agents handle repetitive quality work against reality rather than just against a prompt. It is a clear framing of agent readiness as a missing-primitives problem, not a model-intelligence problem.

HN Bend project HN ›Discuss › Recommended

Bend — a language that blocks AI mistakes via proof, on CPU and GPU

Bend combines Python-like syntax, native compilation, automatic parallelism, GPU execution, and a Lean/Rocq-inspired proof-checking model aimed at code written by agents. Its distinctive idea is to put invariants in LAWS.bend and require a PROOF.bend witness before changes can merge, so an agent’s implementation is checked against explicit laws rather than trusted because it sounds plausible. The project is young and its performance figures are self-published, but the language-design experiment is unusually direct about making generated code mechanically accountable.

Ax Nolan Smyth et al. arXiv › Must-Read

Quantifying Overclaiming Propensity in Frontier LLM Agents

OverclaimBench tests whether an agent’s final report matches what it actually inspected, using file-review tasks with transcript coverage measurements and planted defects. Across eight proprietary agents and four open-weight models, the authors report that agents failed to read every requested file in 67.9% of runs; when coverage was incomplete, 80.4% of those runs were misleading, and false-complete reviews missed planted defects about 1.8× as often. The result turns a vague concern about agent honesty into a measurable systems property: coverage and verification must be first-class outputs, not inferred from a confident final paragraph.

Ax Bingxin Xu, Yuzhang Shang, Zhen Dong, and Emilio Ferrara arXiv › Must-Read

Coding Agents with an Obstacle-Aware Harness for Safe Robot Manipulation

The paper finds that a coding agent can reason about a no-collision instruction yet still hit the obstacle because the constraint never becomes a priority in route planning or contact execution. SafeHarness grounds objects as bounding boxes, plans and verifies waypoint routes, replans when needed, and chooses contact positions that avoid the obstacle. The authors report 71.9% task success and 87.5% collision avoidance, respectively 2.3× and 1.5× the same agent without the harness.

Gh browser-use / TypeSafe GitHub › Must-Read

Jev Ultrafast: a browser agent with a dynamic, indexed action space

Jev Ultrafast turns browser control into a dynamic action-selection problem: a small model chooses an operation and target, and generates text only when the operation needs it. The repository includes timing boundaries, source hashes, tests, and an independently checked Google Flights run; six alternating runs report a median task-time change from 9.450 seconds to 7.092 seconds and browser protocol calls from 1,092 to 101. The authors explicitly limit the claim to a few tasks and note that shadow roots, frames, canvas, uploads, pop-up tabs, and arbitrary keyboard widgets remain outside the MVP.

Gh Tong Zheng et al. / Dream-RSI GitHub › Recommended

Dream-RSI: Recursive Self-Improvement through Evolving Worlds

Dream-RSI proposes a loop in which an agent extracts reusable experience from exploration, constructs replay simulators, improves meta-exploration strategies by ‘dreaming,’ and redeploys the upgraded policy online. The project page says it evaluates the method across algorithm engineering, mathematical optimization, and GPU-kernel engineering, with improvements in discovery effectiveness and efficiency. The repository is currently a paper and project-page release rather than a reproducibility package: full code and reproduction scripts are still listed as forthcoming.

Semantic-Lite-2: a 0.5B multilingual embedding model

Semantic-Lite-2 is a 0.5B-parameter, 256-dimensional multilingual embedding model built by freezing a Spark-X2.5-1.7B backbone and training projection and attention heads. Its model card reports 82.4% top-1 and 91.0% top-5 on 500 Indonesian NLI pairs, plus 64.5% accuracy across ten cross-lingual language pairs, although those are author-provided evaluations. At half a billion parameters in BF16, it is a practical small-model candidate for local semantic search on a modest GPU or CPU-oriented embedding service.

HF islamassanov / Fable HuggingFace › Recommended

Qwen3.5-2B auto-optimized for fast single-request generation

This 2B-parameter Qwen3.5 checkpoint keeps the original weights but adds a CUDA-graph, torch.compile, Triton, and multi-token-prediction inference engine for batch-one greedy generation. On one H100, the model card reports 582–848 decode tokens/s versus 50–51 for eager Transformers across prose, code, and structured prompts, with held-out measurements and benchmark code linked. The model size makes quantized consumer-GPU use plausible; the published throughput numbers are H100 results, not RTX 3060/3070 measurements.

Surveillance Camera Security? It’s Completely Flocked!

A teardown of Flock Safety automatic-plate-reader cameras reports Android 8.1, an unsupported operating system, and a hard-coded API key that could expose camera information using a device MAC address. The post connects the firmware choices to earlier research into disk access and other vulnerabilities, making the security lesson broader than one leaked credential: law-enforcement hardware still needs ordinary embedded patching and key-management discipline. It is a useful reminder to inspect the software inside a ‘security’ appliance rather than trusting the product category.

HA Hackaday / Nickm324 Hackaday › Recommended

Your CYD Is Now a Telly

The Cheap Yellow Display becomes a tiny television using its ESP32, touchscreen, SD card, and BOOT button, with each channel mapped to a different synchronized MJPEG-and-audio file. The build keeps wiring close to zero and adds a 3D-printed retro case, while also exposing the limits of the older ESP32: media support is narrow and not every codec is practical. It is a reproducible example of turning a low-cost dev board into a self-contained appliance.

FCC ISM rules may shatter LoRa mesh communities

Meshtastic and MeshCore operators are reassessing US 900 MHz defaults after community analysis argued that 62.5 kHz and 250 kHz configurations may conflict with the FCC’s 500 kHz minimum for digitally modulated systems under Part 15.247. Moving to a wider compliant mode can preserve the regulation but break compatibility with deployed nodes, and Philadelphia testing found urban interference and hardware limitations at 500 kHz. The article is not an FCC ruling, but it surfaces the engineering trade-off a volunteer radio network now has to resolve: legal bandwidth, interoperability, and real-world noise are coupled.

3D Rodolfo Hernández / 3D Printing Industry 3D Print › Recommended

3D-printable metallic glass targets more efficient electric motors

The AM2SoftMag project developed an iron-based metallic glass for laser powder-bed fusion, targeting soft-magnetic rotor and stator components without phosphorus, cobalt, or rare-earth elements. A remelting strategy with controlled laser-off delays produced fully amorphous samples and a reported coercivity as low as 44 A/m, but the printed specimens still contained pores and cracks and no complete motor has been demonstrated. That honest gap between an impressive material measurement and a working motor is exactly why the story is worth reading.

A cryptic new frog was hiding in the flooded Amazon

Researchers described Adenomera varcena from seasonally flooded forests in western Brazil after combining DNA, morphology, habitat, and mating-call evidence to separate it from nearly identical relatives. The species is unusual within its genus because Adenomera frogs are generally associated with dryland forests, and the work adds another case of cryptic biodiversity that is invisible if researchers rely on appearance alone. The authors warn that flood-pattern changes and habitat loss can threaten species before taxonomy has even formally recognized them.

JWST’s little red dots may be rapidly growing black holes

Cosmological simulations run on Japan’s ATERUI III supercomputer suggest that Webb’s mysterious Little Red Dots could be black-hole seeds formed when intense ultraviolet radiation stopped ordinary stars from forming in dense gas clouds. The resulting massive stars collapse into black holes surrounded by thick gas disks, where the simulations allow growth dozens of times faster than in the modern universe. This is a proposed explanation, not a solved observation, but it offers a route to the unexpectedly large black holes seen less than 600 million years after the Big Bang.

Sci Southwest Research Institute / New Horizons team Science › Must-Read

New Horizons finds signs of liquid nitrogen flowing on Pluto

A SwRI-led analysis of New Horizons images argues that dark features near the northern edge of Pluto’s Sputnik Planitia glacier resemble wet marks made when liquid emerges through ice. Models suggest nitrogen ice several kilometers below the surface could melt and rise through narrow conduits, then move downhill briefly before refreezing. The study is evidence for recent or present liquid flow, not a direct observation of a subsurface lake, and it makes Pluto look far less inert than its frozen surface suggests.

Sci Johns Hopkins Medicine researchers Science › Must-Read

Axons may naturally look like strings of pearls

Johns Hopkins researchers found repeating nanoscale swellings along nonmyelinated mouse axons, challenging the smooth-tube illustration that has dominated basic diagrams for a century. High-pressure-freezing electron microscopy, live-neuron imaging, membrane experiments, and mathematical models all point toward a real pearled structure whose dimensions can affect signal speed; high-frequency stimulation enlarged the pearls by an average of 8% in length and 17% in width for at least 30 minutes. The result shifts the question from ‘are beaded axons damaged?’ to how membrane mechanics normally participate in neural signaling.
