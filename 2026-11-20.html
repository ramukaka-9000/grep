RoCoKV: uncertainty- and redundancy-aware KV-cache compression

RoCoKV targets the growing KV-cache cost of long reasoning traces, where repeated checks and corrections can occupy memory without adding much value. Its approach combines uncertainty-guided adaptive pruning with redundancy awareness, aiming to retain more cache when the model is uncertain and reclaim space from lower-utility tokens. The paper is a timely serving-side idea for long-context and reasoning workloads.

Evolve Where It Matters: coverage-guided self-evolution of agent skills

SkillPlumber treats agent-skill improvement as an instruction-level diagnosis problem: did a run fail because an instruction was wrong, or because the agent did not follow it? This is a concrete evaluation frame for teams maintaining reusable skills and playbooks.

MCP ToolRank 33M: a compact model for ranking tools

This 33.36M-parameter sentence-similarity model ranks English MCP and API tools from names and descriptions. Its model card reports CPU INT8 short-query latency of 3.53 ms and fresh-API holdout nDCG@10 of 90.43%, with the evaluation and limits documented. A genuinely small, runnable model aimed at a narrow but increasingly important agent problem: choosing the right tool from a catalog.

A 4.1M-parameter methane-plume detector sized for edge compute

This 4.13M-parameter U-Net uses a MobileNetV2 encoder to segment methane plumes in 10 m Sentinel-2 imagery, with a plume-free reference image required for comparison. The model card includes a measured held-out test split, inference files, and important caveats: it is a demonstration rather than a validated monitoring product, and full-swath onboard operation still needs tiling and more negative data. Its tiny footprint and explicit limitations make it an unusually useful edge-vision release.

Voyager 1 makes history reaching one light-day from Earth

NASA reports that Voyager 1 crossed the one-light-day distance on November 18, 2026: roughly 16.1 billion miles (25.9 billion kilometres) from Earth after nearly five decades in flight. The milestone is about distance, not travel time; even a radio signal now takes a full day to reach the spacecraft. NASA also notes the probe is currently sending a carrier signal while engineers investigate a pause in science and engineering data.

A 6-million-year-old whale may have had the most crooked face in mammal history

A fossil pygmy sperm whale from Peru, Scaphokogia huachafo, had an exceptionally downturned snout and an asymmetrical skull that researchers interpret as an adaptation for feeding along the seafloor rather than post-mortem damage. The animal lived roughly six million years ago, offering a striking example of how specialized and unusual ancient marine mammals could become.
