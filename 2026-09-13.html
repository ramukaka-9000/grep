Researchers find a Wordle strategy that wins 99% of the time

A Binghamton University team used Shannon entropy to choose Wordle guesses that maximally reduce the remaining answer space, rather than simply selecting common letters or likely answers. In simulations the information-gain strategy solved 99% of puzzles versus 90% for the common-letter baseline, an accessible demonstration of active information gathering.

66-million-year-old feather in dinosaur poop may reveal why birds survived the asteroid

Micro-CT scans of a fossilized coprolite revealed feathers, fish scales, and bird bones from a hesperornithiform swallowed near the end of the Cretaceous. The primitive, less-insulating plumage may help explain why that aquatic bird lineage died out while the ancestors of modern birds survived the asteroid-driven impact winter.

Scientists capture two DNA strands zipping together for the first time

Atomic-force microscopy and simulations captured DNA helices aligning groove-for-groove even though both molecules are negatively charged. Divalent metal ions act as molecular bridges, providing experimental support for the two-decade-old DNA-zipper model and revealing sequence-dependent pairing hotspots.

Mercury’s surface reveals a surprisingly fiery past

A new calibration using laboratory glass beads estimates that silicon dioxide makes up about 37% of Mercury’s surface, up to 25% less than earlier estimates. The low value points to ancient lava from a more deeply and extensively melted mantle, while the Moon provided a test case because it has both remote-sensing data and returned rock samples.
