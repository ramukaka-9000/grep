Whistle: speech to text in 16.9 MB

Whistle is a 16.9 MB on-device ASR model that loads into Cactus Needle's CPU engine: 16 kHz mono, up to 30 seconds, seven languages, word timestamps, and a speech embedding. The authors report 11.1 ms to first token and 1,319 tok/s decode on an M4 Pro CPU, with lower WER than Whisper base on LibriSpeech clean/other, SPGISpeech, Earnings-22, and FLEURS, while Whisper base still leads TED-LIUM, AMI, and MLS at 145 MB. It shares Needle's Simple Attention blocks, adds gated cross-attention, and ships prebuilt for seventeen targets including the browser. Treat the benches as vendor-reported; weights are on Hugging Face as Cactus-Compute/whistle.

Step 5 Preview: 1M-context MoE on OpenRouter

Step 5 Preview showed up on OpenRouter on 8 October as StepFun's agentic flagship: sparse MoE with 27B active and 600B total parameters, 1.0M context, listed at $1 / $2.70 per million tokens with $0.05 cache reads. OpenRouter's page positions it for long software and document work, especially finance, and currently routes only to StepFun. Weighted average input is far below list because of cache hits; P50 throughput is about 42 tok/s. This is a hosted preview listing, not an open-weight drop.

DuckLake: lakehouse metadata in SQL, data in Parquet

DuckLake keeps lakehouse metadata in an ordinary SQL catalog (PostgreSQL, SQLite, MySQL, or DuckDB) and stores table data as Parquet, with snapshots, time travel, schema evolution, and multi-table ACID. The MIT DuckDB extension is the client; v1.0 in April 2026 promised backward compatibility, and the repo is still moving (3.1k stars, commits through 8 October). Several production users are listed on ducklake.select. It is a format plus extension, not a hosted warehouse.

HN Samsung Labs (via Hacker News) HN ›Discuss › Recommended

LittleBit: sub-1-bit LLM compression via latent factorization

LittleBit (NeurIPS 2025) factorizes dense weights into low-rank latents, binarizes them, and restores scale, targeting 1.0 down to 0.1 bits per weight without changing the inference architecture. LittleBit-2 (ICML 2026) adds Joint-ITQ initialization behind --use_itq; it does not add inference cost. The open code supports OPT, Llama 2/3, Phi-4, Qwen2.5/Qwen3, Gemma 2/3, and QwQ. License is CC BY-NC 4.0; a binary-unpacker fix landed 8 October.

Ax Drew T. Nguyen, William Fithian arXiv ›Discuss › Must-Read

A statistical look at METR's AI time-horizon plot

METR's 50% time horizon maps AI success to the human time of software tasks. Nguyen and Fithian recompute it on 228 tasks and 26 AIs with splines and item-response theory, dropping the assumption that AI difficulty is linear in log human time. Their spline is nearly flat from 2 to 30 minutes and closer to linear elsewhere, so a jump from 3 minutes to 30 minutes is much easier than 30 minutes to 5 hours despite the same 10× multiplier. They argue new horizon benches should ship the diagnostic plots, not just a single number.

Ax Wang, Ye, Barr, Huo arXiv ›Discuss › Recommended

Cadence: schedule coding-agent monitors by execution health

Fixed-interval or heuristic monitors either miss bad agent trajectories or burn tokens on healthy ones. Cadence uses two intervention levels, advisory versus replacement, and tightens or relaxes inspection from that health signal. On 300 SWE-bench Lite tasks it reports +76 resolved tasks on mini-swe-agent (+25.33 points) and +47 on Moatless (+15.67), with competitive token use versus other monitors. Author-reported; the useful idea is adaptive cadence, not a new coding model.

Agent Memory Repo: a git wiki for agent memory

Cognition's Agent Memory Repo spec puts live, linked memories in a separate git repo: clone, grep or follow links, update without a human in the loop, and push after every edit. The MIT repo is the spec plus a skill installer for Devin, Claude Code, and others; your memories never go in the public spec tree. It is a convention, not a hosted memory product.

Gh ymcrcat (Show HN) GitHub ›Discuss › Recommended

rGPU: keep Python local, hold tensors on a remote GPU

rGPU is Apache-2.0 remote compute: a PyTorch device so tensors live on a remote NVIDIA box, including from a Mac with no CUDA, plus a CUDA shim for existing Linux binaries. pip install rgpu, then rgpu-run over SSH; torch.ones(..., device="rgpu") is the smoke test. Neither protocol authenticates or encrypts, so the docs want localhost bind plus SSH, and the CUDA server on port 9713 firewalled. Small repo (about 41 stars); treat it as a working remote-device experiment, not a cluster fabric.

MinerU2.5-Flash-0.8B: faster document parsing on Qwen3.5

This 0.85B document parser keeps MinerU2.5-Pro's protocol on Qwen3.5-0.8B (18 linear-attention plus 6 full-attention layers) instead of the official 1.2B Qwen2-VL. The card reports 95.41 OmniDocBench overall versus 95.72 official, and 3.45 pages/s on one A100 vLLM instance versus 2.47, or 4.27 with two instances on the same GPU. Needs vLLM 0.24, mineru-vl-utils 2.0, and transformers 5, with an explicit pixel budget. BF16 weights are about 0.9B params; a 3060 should run a quantized copy, but the published speed numbers are A100, not consumer GPUs.

HF ajhendel / MojoLearn HuggingFace ›Discuss › Recommended

MojoLearn 162M: bitwise-identical training on three GPU vendors

A 162M Llama-style decoder was trained twice from the same init on 2.62B FineWeb-Edu tokens, once on each of two routes through NVIDIA, AMD, and Apple GPUs. The card says the runs are bitwise identical at all 5,000 optimizer steps, including summed gradients and per-shard losses, with matching SHA-256 checkpoints. Held-out loss is 3.265 nats/token; 0-shot HellaSwag is 28.0% (acc_norm 29.6%). It is an FP32 reproducibility artifact, not a production chat model; Apache-2.0 weights, about 650 MB, run on CPU.

OSC 7501: a terminal protocol for program status

Hashimoto specifies OSC 7501 so a program can tell the terminal it is idle, working, blocked on the user, finished, or failed, with structured fields instead of window-title heuristics. The post argues agent inboxes that scrape screens or process trees are guessing at state the program already knows. He has implementations in libghostty, Rex, and short proofs in Terraform, Claude Code, Codex, and Homebrew. The spec is generic; skip the AI section if you only care about builds and deploys.

The world's smallest TV console plays Doom

An ESP32-S3, battery, and flex PCB fit inside a 300-to-75-ohm TV antenna plug and radiate PAL VHF so a television can play Doom. Composite comes from a resistor-ladder DAC with a chroma phase LUT; the ESP clock generator supplies the carrier; an Xbox pad talks Bluetooth. The write-up measures about 120 mA with BLE, which ruled out earbud cells, and hardware is on GitHub as pstiasny/tv-doom. Designed for PAL VHF; NTSC or ATSC would be another radio problem.

Ad Weeb Labs (via Adafruit) Adafruit ›Discuss › Recommended

DSPi: USB audio DSP firmware for Pico and Pico 2

DSPi turns a Raspberry Pi Pico or Pico 2 into a USB sound card with room correction, active crossovers, parametric EQ, time alignment, loudness, and headphone crossfeed. Both chips run at a fixed 307.2 MHz so PIO dividers stay integer at 44.1, 48, and 96 kHz; the RP2040 needs a small voltage bump, while the RP2350's FPU allows 11 inputs and 9 outputs versus 7 and 5. Each output can switch S/PDIF or I2S at runtime, pins store in flash across 10 presets, and Core 1 drives a PDM subwoofer. GPL v3, standard Pico C/C++ toolchain.

Ad Zoltan Doczi / RTL-SDR.com (via Adafruit) Adafruit ›Discuss › Recommended

ESP32-SDR: gapless turbo spectra and real I/Q

ESP32-SDR used undocumented radio tricks but could only burst spectra with about 42 ms gaps because USB cannot carry 80 MS/s I/Q. A ring buffer across three SRAM banks keeps the dump engine running: turbo mode at 80 MS/s and 256 bins reports 1,295 gapless spectra per second, characterized with a Signal Hound VSG60. The same buffer enables real I/Q at up to 250 ksps into SDR++ via sdrpp-esp-sdr-source; an FM broadcast was mixed up with a moRFeus. The 80 MHz waterfall is display data only, not a demodulated 80 MHz IQ stream.

HN Natural Systems (via Hacker News) HN ›Discuss › Recommended

ETH-68: Ethernet audio for Linux at 3.62 ms round trip

ETH-68 is a 1U STM32H7 box that emulates a netJACK1 master: 6 balanced TRS ins, 8 outs, MIDI, Burr-Brown PCM3168A, 48 and 96 kHz. jack_delay with a loopback cable measures 3.620 ms round trip at 48 kHz and 64 samples, matching an RME HDSPe AIO Pro at 48 kHz and beating it by 0.33 ms at 96 kHz. Units share an HF clock over BNC and a UDP sync so extra boxes add channels without extra latency. Linux-first; JACK-Router on macOS is still broken after 10.15.

A 5.3-million-year-old whale necropolis in the Diamantina Zone

A Nature paper reports a whale-fall field about 1,200 km long on the Diamantina Zone floor in the southeastern Indian Ocean, 4,616 to 7,001 m deep. The authors count five living whale-fall communities and 476 fossil cetaceans, including extant and extinct beaked whales, with isotopic dates back to at least 5.3 million years. Living carcasses host brittle stars, bone-boring worms, and chemosynthetic bivalves. Published 10 June 2026; HN is circulating the PDF this week.

ESA ESA / Jesse T. Coburn et al. ESA ›Discuss › Recommended

Solar Orbiter traces magnetic switchbacks to the corona

Switchbacks are S-shaped kinks in the solar wind. Coburn et al., in Nature Astronomy, use Solar Orbiter particle data plus SDO imaging to argue they start with interchange reconnection at the Sun, then waves and turbulence take over once the structure is in space. ESA says no other craft has both the proximity and the instruments for that split. The claim is about origin and evolution, not a new space-weather forecast product.

A 15-year search for a band that charted once and vanished

Hussain spent 15 years hunting Salvage, whose "Searchlights" / "Electric" CD single hit number 47 on the UK Independent Singles Chart in June 2006 and then disappeared under generic search terms and a Flash site. France's Téléscaphe was an underwater cable car for one season

The Téléscaphe opened near Les Goudes, Marseille, in June 1967: four bell-shaped six-seat submarines on a 500 m cable at about 10 m depth, frogmen on safety, 12 francs a ride. Wikipedia records about 31,000 passengers, a maiden-voyage slot on the Our World satellite broadcast, then closure after one season on cost, plus an incident that put a cabin on its side. Shore bull wheels remain. The TIL is right on the hardware; treat "one year" as one season.

Stone Age 'batons' look like rope factories

A 21 September 2026 Smithsonian feature follows archaeologists reconstructing fiber tech that rarely survives: thread, twine, and rope. A mammoth-ivory lochstab from Hohle Fels, more than 35,000 years old, was first read as a prestige object; Nicholas Conard's team now treats the four holes as a rope-making tool, shown with cattail fiber in a 2024 Science Advances experiment with Veerle Rots. The piece is a survey of that work, not a new excavation. Fiber still has to be inferred from tools and wear.
