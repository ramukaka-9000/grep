AI finally beat the best Stratego player in history — and did it on a budget

Ataraxos, from CMU, MIT, NYU and Stanford, beat Pim Niemeijer — arguably the greatest Stratego player ever — 15-1 with four draws. Stratego's massive hidden information unfolding over long time scales had stumped even DeepMind, and this team did it on 16 GPUs and a few thousand dollars of training. ds4: run frontier open weights locally, from the creator of Redis

Salvatore Sanfilippo ships DwarfStar 4, a narrow C inference engine for high-memory Mac, CUDA and ROCm boxes targeting DeepSeek V4/V4.1 Flash, GLM 5.x and Qwen3.8 Flash Next. One stack with CLI, OpenAI/Anthropic-compatible APIs and a native agent — from the engineer who built Redis.

The first packet sent via RFC1149 avian carrier is up for auction at Christie's

The physical payload from the 2001 April Fools' IP-over-avian-carrier experiment — a tiny card carried by pigeon — is being auctioned in Christie's fine printed books and manuscripts sale. The 2023 retransmission run measured throughput in the same ballpark the original paper predicted, packet loss and all. Networking history as literal artifact: the only protocol whose mean time to recovery depends on whether the router got eaten by a hawk.

Sharks can hear nearly 250 feet away and pinpoint the source

Experiments with free-swimming blacktip sharks off Southeast Florida show they detect low-frequency sounds from nearly 250 feet (~75 m) away — and sharply change direction relative to the source, meaning they localize it rather than merely sense it. This is the first direct field evidence of how far shark hearing actually reaches in the ocean, and it reframes boat engines, shore activity and even waders as acoustic beacons sharks navigate by.

This giant stick insect fooled scientists for decades

Re-examining Australian stick insects of the genus Anchiale, a University of Sydney team described two new species — one laying extraordinary eggs with a lid-like structure never before recorded in the group — and restored a third species that had been misidentified for decades. These were sitting in some of Australia's most closely studied environments. A crisp demonstration that 'known' biodiversity is often just 'unrevised' biodiversity.

Caltech physicists finally measure a quantum energy ladder predicted 40 years ago

Using laser-trapped strontium atoms in a quantum simulator, researchers recreated two quantum critical points and measured the excitation-energy ratios conformal field theory predicted four decades ago — the rungs of the ladder, at the predicted spacing. The technique now opens a way to probe quantum systems where nobody yet knows the answer. Universality means boiling water and demagnetizing magnets obey the same math; this is that math, caught on a ruler.

At 10:16 CEST on 30 September 2026 Mars began Mars year 39 — the planet completes an orbit every 687 Earth days, so its previous year started in January 2025. ESA's explainer also maps the next few Martian New Years: August 2028, July 2030, May 2032. Anyone planning a Mars settlement calendar now knows the leap-year negotiations come first.

Venice's failed war against Constantinople led to the first bond market

A review of 'A Fabulous Debt' traces how Venice's disastrous 12th-century war against Byzantium forced the republic to borrow compulsorily from its own citizens — and how those forced loans, traded, pooled and securitized, became history's first public bond market. Financial innovation as war fallout, eight centuries before anyone printed a Treasury note. The cultural connection HN loved: your pension fund descends from a lost siege.
