Restricting AI agents in mobile operating systems platforms from capturing data

Signal says it will block operating-system AI assistants from reading its screen on iOS 27 and on Android versions where it can detect such capture, with an in-app option to allow screenshots. The post makes a concrete privacy point for agent builders: end-to-end encryption cannot protect message content once an OS-level assistant reads it from the display.

Inkling 2 Mini

Thinking Machines Lab describes Inkling 2 Mini as a 26.4B-parameter MoE with 3.4B active parameters that loops through its middle layers three times per token. The lab reports gains on coding and reasoning benchmarks and says its NVFP4/MXFP8 weights fit in about 20GB; the results are vendor-reported, and the stated memory footprint is not a fit for the tiny-model channel.

Anthropic describes two theoretical-physics calculations by Claude, including a result about a black-hole phase transition and a bound on a method used to test candidate quantum-gravity theories. Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights

The authors report that an open 27B model can edit its own weights using forward-pass reasoning, without gradients or a conventional training run, and improve on held-out problems after checking edits against practice cases. Their paper also reports strong dependence on model scale and task setup, so this is an intriguing research demonstration rather than evidence of general self-improvement.

TideCache: Efficient Hybrid LLM Serving with Transparent Cache Management and Layer-Wise Streaming

TideCache manages the distinct KV-cache needs of hybrid models by dynamically sharing GPU memory and streaming selected attention-layer prefixes from host memory. The authors report up to 2.04× as many agent turns, 20–59% lower median per-token latency, and 1.40–3.05× better agent time-to-first-token across seven GB300 and GH200 configurations against an SGLang/HiCache baseline. Those are paper results on specific high-end systems, but the resource-management design is directly relevant to serving long-running agents.

Verify the Verifier: Specification-Grounded LLM Cross-Checking for Bugs That Escape UVM Scoreboards

This work asks whether a passing hardware regression actually satisfies its specification, rather than merely matching what its scoreboard checks. In experiments with 564 inserted bugs across four OpenTitan IPs, the authors identify 68 candidate scoreboard escapes and confirm 41 with specification-grounded checks. It is a useful example of applying language models as a cross-check over test evidence, not as a substitute for formal assertions.

ettin-17m-thinking-ruler: a 17M model that estimates how much reasoning a prompt needs

This 16.9M-parameter Ettin encoder is fine-tuned to estimate the relative reasoning length a prompt may require, potentially helping a system allocate test-time compute. Mamba-370M, 90% semi-structured sparsity: ELSA (official code)

This 423M-parameter Mamba-370M checkpoint applies 90% row-wise semi-structured sparsity using the ELSA pruning code and can be loaded with standard Transformers. The page publishes reproducible settings and evaluation numbers, but reports a substantial quality drop from the dense baseline and does not establish faster inference on ordinary hardware; it is a useful, candid pruning artifact rather than a ready-made speedup.

A KIM-1 tape conversion program for modern computers

Hans Otten's Free Pascal port of KIMTAPE converts KIM-1 and SYM-1 cassette recordings into files and back, and builds on Windows, Linux and macOS. Its decoder uses a band-pass filter and FM discriminator rather than simple zero-crossing counts, tolerates speed variation and distorted waveforms, and can report or repair uncertain characters while checking the checksum.

The 20c is a 6502-based modular learning computer

The open-source 20c breaks a 6502-family computer into chip-level modules for the CPU, memory, buses, clock and I/O, making the architecture easier to experiment with than a breadboard full of loose jumpers. Swapping modules can approximate systems such as a Commodore 64 or Atari 2600, and an Arduino Mega connector can serve as a protocol analyzer.

Camp Ready Raspberry Pi Cyberdeck

This rugged, battery-powered Raspberry Pi 5 build packs a touchscreen, 1TB NVMe, offline Wikipedia and maps, GPS/PPS timekeeping, SDR, educational media and local Wi-Fi into a largely custom 3D-printed enclosure. Its maker reports more than 24 hours of battery life and roughly 6.5W draw with the screen off, useful constraints for anyone designing an offline field computer.

Curiosity Spots Drifting Twilight Clouds

NASA's Curiosity rover captured post-sunset clouds over Gale Crater on Oct. 31, 2026, during its 5,061st Martian sol. These twilight clouds appear briefly during each fall season there, and the rover's navigation cameras recorded them in a striking panoramic mosaic.

A 3-million-year-old child just made human evolution far more complicated

A study published in npj Palaeoecosystems dates the Taung Child to about 2.96–3.03 million years ago and compares its development with other southern African hominin fossils. The authors argue that some fossils traditionally grouped as Australopithecus africanus may represent a more diverse set of lineages; the result is a reassessment of how the evidence is classified, not a settled rewrite of human ancestry.

CERN scientists finally find evidence of a particle decay so rare it took 40 years to detect

The upgraded LHCb experiment reports 3.2-sigma evidence for the decay of a beauty meson into two muons, a process predicted to occur only about once in ten billion beauty particles. That is evidence, not the five-sigma threshold normally required for a particle-physics discovery; the result gives researchers a new way to test the Standard Model as more data arrive.
