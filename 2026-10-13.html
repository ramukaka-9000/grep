Janas streams oversized language models from SSD

Janas is an experimental C inference engine that streams model experts from SSD, with CPU/GPU support and speculative decoding. Its author reports 24 generated tokens per second from Qwen3-Next-80B on a 32 GB laptop—versus 2.8 tok/s with llama.cpp in the same comparison—and publishes results across 13 models; those are developer-run benchmarks, not an independent evaluation.

X-AgentRed explores harmful paths across connected services

The framework builds a graph of tool effects and preconditions, then uses execution feedback to find cross-service action sequences that can produce harmful outcomes from benign requests. Across four frontier agents, the authors report 1.77× the harmful-path rate and 2.99× average tool coverage versus their baseline. The findings are an author-run red-team evaluation, but the graph-and-execution approach targets compositional risks that single-tool tests can miss.

AI coding agents increase output, while review absorbs the gains

A study using engineering activity across more than 700 firms and 700,000 employees found that adopting coding agents coincided with 30% more lines of code, 20% more commits, and 23% more pull requests, while review time and revision requests also rose. The authors find little evidence that firms increased software output or reduced employment, suggesting the coding-stage speedup can be absorbed downstream. These are firm-level observational results, not a claim that every team experiences the same effect.

QGIS proposes a pause on AI-generated contributions

A proposal in QGIS's public enhancement-process repository calls for a temporary moratorium on AI-generated contributions while the project works out community policy. It makes the governance question concrete for maintainers: how to handle code whose provenance, review burden, or authorship may be difficult to establish. This is a proposal in the project's workflow, not evidence of a permanent ban.

A Wi-Fi 7 router becomes a 320 Msps software-defined radio

The project replaces firmware on a router built around MediaTek's MT7996 radio and streams raw 14-bit IQ samples to a host, with a browser spectrum and waterfall display. The repository documents 40–320 Msps capture over 10 GbE and says its firmware is receive-only; it also warns that installation replaces the stock operating system and that restoration requires a separate raw-NAND writer. This is an advanced hardware hack, not a casual router firmware update.

Ivy AI runs a fully offline voice assistant on an ESP32-S3

Ivy chains wake-word detection, speech recognition, a 19.7-million-parameter 4-bit language model, and speech synthesis on an ESP32-S3 with 8 MB of PSRAM; Wi-Fi is used only for clock sync. The project's measured results show the limits as well as the feat: transcription takes longer than real time, and a rephrased test question was answered correctly less often than the wording used in training. The code, model measurements, hardware requirements, and known limitations are documented in the repository.

Read eight analog channels through one Arduino multiplexer

An NXP HEF4052BP dual 4-channel analog switch routes eight inputs to two Arduino Uno ADC pins, with a simple serial monitor and potentiometers standing in for sensors. The tutorial includes wiring and code, including a settling delay and a discarded conversion after switching channels. It explicitly notes that the inputs are sampled in pairs rather than simultaneously and flags source impedance, voltage limits, and settling as accuracy considerations.

Paperboy pushes Game Boy graphics to 60 fps on e-paper

Paperboy emulates original Game Boy games on M5Stack e-paper devices, drawing frames directly to the panel rather than relying on full-screen refreshes. The firmware provides sound, touch controls, a ROM picker, and save states, and the repository documents builds for both the ESP32-S3 M5PaperS3 and the original ESP32 M5Paper. The 60-fps result is the project's reported display capability, constrained by the panel's partial-refresh behavior.

Antarctic sea ice reaches its third-lowest winter maximum on record

NASA and the National Snow and Ice Data Center estimate that Antarctic sea ice reached its 2026 maximum on September 14, covering 17.59 million square kilometers—the third-lowest maximum in the satellite record. The record began in 1978, and NASA cautions that year-to-year variability makes it too early to know whether the recent decline is a lasting shift or a statistical fluctuation.

A Moon-like mass of anorthosite stands out in Madagascar

An astronaut photograph from the International Space Station shows the Saririaky anorthosite massif in southern Madagascar, a bright rock formation resembling the Moon's highlands. The exposed body covers about 100 square kilometers and is at least 600 million years old; NASA links its geology to ancient continental collisions. Anorthosite is not unique to the Moon, but this unusually clear terrestrial outcrop makes the lunar connection vivid.

SpaceX's next station cargo mission carries brain and crop experiments

NASA's CRS-35 resupply mission is carrying several studies for the International Space Station, including lab-grown brain tissue, fungus-infected plants, and tracking of antibiotic-resistant bacteria. The experiments compare spaceflight effects with Earth controls and test whether a compact lab reader can measure health biomarkers in orbit. The results could inform both long-duration crew health and crop disease management on Earth.
